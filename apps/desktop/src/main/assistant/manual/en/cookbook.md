The Cookbook holds your recipes and the world's cuisines from packs. One page carries four things: the list of recipes, one recipe, the cuisines from packs, and the nutrition.

How to use it:

1. "Recipes": "Search" looks through a title, a tag or an ingredient — "what can I make with…", say. A row carries "Open", "Cook", "Edit", "Delete" and the favourite mark.
2. "New recipe" asks for "Title", "Course" ("Breakfast", "Starter", "Soup", "Main course", "Side", "Salad", "Dessert", "Baking", "Drink", "Preserve", "Other"), "Servings", "Prep (min)", "Cook (min)", "Ingredients — one per line", "Steps — one per line", "Tags" and "Notes". Each ingredient line is read as amount, unit, name, then a note after the comma.
3. An open recipe carries "Servings" with "Fewer servings"/"More servings" — the amounts are recalculated. "Cook" shows one step across the whole screen: left and right arrows change the step, Escape closes it, and the screen stays awake while it is open.
4. "The world's cuisines" are the recipes inside installed packs; "Search the pack" looks through them, and "Save to my cookbook" copies one recipe over.
5. "Nutrition" is computed from a dataset pack holding `foods.json`, per serving: "Energy", "Protein", "Carbohydrate", "Fat", "Fibre", "Sugars" and "Salt". With no pack it says "No foods pack is installed".

Recipes live in the profile, on this computer. Settings, on the "Cookbook" card, chooses "Metric (g, ml)" or "Kitchen (spoons, cups)" units.
