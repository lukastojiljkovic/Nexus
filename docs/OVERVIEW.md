# Nexus — A Plain-Language Guide

> **For anyone, technical or not.** This explains what Nexus is, who it's for, how
> you use it, and why it's different — in everyday language, with no programming
> terms. For the full feature list see [SPECIFICATION.md](SPECIFICATION.md); for what
> is built so far see [STATUS.md](STATUS.md).

---

## What is Nexus?

Most people run their lives across a dozen different apps: one for to-dos, another
for notes, a calendar, a budgeting spreadsheet, a fitness tracker, flashcards for
studying, something for opening files, and a drawer full of documents — ID cards,
passports, insurance — that nothing reminds them about until they've expired.

Everything is scattered. Each app wants its own account and stores your data on
someone else's servers. Nothing talks to anything else.

**Nexus brings all of it into one place.** It's a single app that handles your
tasks, calendar, notes, studying, finances, fitness, files, and more — where
everything works together. One home screen. One calendar. One reminder system. One
search box. One consistent, calm look.

The idea isn't that any single piece is magical on its own — plenty of apps do
to-dos or notes. The value is that they're **finally in one place and connected**,
so you stop juggling and stop copying information from one app to another.

We describe it as **"the app you open first in the morning, and the only organizer
you need."**

## Who is it for?

- **Students first.** The creator built it for himself during exam season — juggling

- **Then professionals** — architects, developers, freelancers — and really anyone
  who feels buried under scattered obligations.
- Over time, tailored "packs" for specific professions, all built on the same core.

## The three ideas that make Nexus different

**1. It works without the internet — always.**
Your data lives on your own computer. Every core feature works with the internet
completely off. Online syncing (using it across your phone and laptop) will come
later as a *bonus*, never as a requirement. There's even a mode where no server is
ever involved at all.

**2. Your privacy is built in, not promised.**
Private notes are locked so securely that **not even the people who make Nexus could
read them**. This isn't a policy on a website — it's how the app is built. And you
can always export **all** of your data and take it with you. Nexus never holds your
information hostage.

**3. You only see what you need.**
Nexus has many parts, but you don't get all of them dumped on you. A short, friendly
setup at the start tailors the app to you, and everything else stays tucked away
until you choose to turn it on. You can even keep your **personal** and **business**
lives in completely separate spaces.

## What you can do with it today

Right now, Nexus is a **working desktop program** (for Windows, Mac, and Linux) that
you install like any other app. Fifteen parts of it are built and usable, and the
founder has been running his own week in it every day since early August 2026.

**Getting in.** The first time you open it, a short four-screen setup asks your name,
lets you pick a **light ("Dan")** or **dark ("Noć")** look, and asks a few questions
about your life so it can switch on only the parts you'll actually use. Your account
lives on your computer and is locked with a passcode — no server is involved at any
point. You can keep several accounts on one machine, and split your life into a
**personal** and a **business** profile that never see each other's data.

**Kontrolna tabla (home).** What you see first: a greeting, today's date, and a grid
of cards you arrange yourself — today's agenda, upcoming tasks, documents about to
expire, exam countdowns, habit streaks, study minutes. You can have several named
dashboards, set your own background image, and click anything to jump to it.

**Zadaci (tasks).** To-dos with priorities, due dates, sub-tasks, lists, sections,
recurrence, dependencies, templates and attachments. View them as a checklist, a
board with columns you configure, cards or a calendar. Ready-made smart lists —
"Danas", "Sledećih 7 dana", "Hitno", "Kasni" — plus batch edits and undo on anything
you delete.

**Kalendar (calendar).** Month, week, day and agenda views; events you can drag and
resize; repeating events; birthdays; semester dates. It also holds the **document
tracker** — you tell it when your ID card, passport, driver's licence, car
registration or bank cards expire, and it shows each one green, amber or red, and
reminds you weeks ahead. You can import a calendar file from another app and export
yours as one.

**Beleške (notes).** Rich notes with folders, categories, tags and attachments;
links between notes with automatic backlinks; version history you can roll back;
templates; and flashcards written straight inside a note.

**Privatno (private notes).** A vault whose contents are encrypted so that nobody
but you — not even us, and not a future server — can read them. It has its own
Recovery Kit, separate from your account, so losing one doesn't lose the other.

**Datoteke (files).** Preview PDFs, images, text, code, spreadsheets and archives
inside Nexus, without opening anything else.

**Učenje (study).** Subjects and exams with countdowns; flashcard decks with
spaced-repetition scheduling that decides *when* to review each card; cloze and
step-by-step problem cards; maths typeset properly; mixed practice sessions across
decks; an exam planner that works backwards from the exam date, puts real study
blocks on your calendar and re-plans when you fall behind; and honest statistics.
You can import an Anki deck.

**Finansije (finance).** Accounts, transactions, categories, budgets and
subscriptions — including pausing one. Each currency is tracked **separately** and
never converted, because a rate the app can't verify would silently misstate money.

**Navike (habits).** Habits with daily or weekly quotas, a calendar of what you did,
and gentle streaks that don't punish you for one missed day.

**Fitnes (nutrition and training).** A food catalogue of 426 entries from
public-domain sources with the provenance of every number recorded, meal logging
and daily totals — and the training half beside it: 230 exercises you can filter
by equipment, muscle or movement, sessions logged against routines with „prošli
put" beside every exercise, personal records and a strength trend worked out from
the sets themselves, and body measurements whose energy figure is **measured from
your own weight trend and what you ate** rather than guessed from a formula.

**Fokus (focus).** One timer for the whole app — a Pomodoro that keeps running while
you navigate, and that records the same session whether you started it here or in
Učenje.

**Alatke (tools).** A drawer of small everyday tools: unit converters for length,
mass, volume, temperature, area, speed and data; percentages; VAT; a loan
calculator; unit-price comparison.

**Stručne alatke (professional tools).** A second drawer, off until you ask for
it, holding eighteen toolkits for the work people actually do — building,
accounting, photography, law, transport, kitchen, sound, joinery, farming,
training, teaching, print design, property, events, engineering, text, business,
software. You pick yours during setup by answering what turns up in your week,
and change the selection any time. A tool that serves several trades exists
once and appears in each of them. Anything whose answer somebody could be hurt
by carries a visible notice that travels with the number when you copy it out,
and anything that depends on a rule that can change — a tax rate, a deadline, a
safety factor — is typed by you rather than assumed by us.

**Tabla (canvas).** An infinite board for drawing, diagramming and arranging ideas,
in Serbian, with your notes, tasks and events placed on it as live cards that stay in
sync with the real thing.

**Podešavanja (settings).** Themes, accent colour, which modules are on, per-module
preferences, keyboard shortcuts you can remap, notification appetite and quiet hours,
profile management, scheduled encrypted backups, and the third-party notices.

**Across all of it.** One search box that finds anything anywhere. Reminders you can
snooze. Undo on deletes. And a full export of **everything** into a single sealed
archive you can carry away or restore — including someone else's archive merged into
yours.

Everything is saved on your computer, works with the network off, and looks like one
thoughtfully designed app throughout.

## What's coming (the bigger picture)

Nexus is being built one complete piece at a time. What is being worked on now:

- **The web app** — the same program, in a browser and on a phone, built from this
  same work rather than started over. The groundwork exists; what a person can
  actually open does not yet.
- **Syncing across devices** — so your laptop and phone hold the same data. The
  machinery underneath is built and tested, and as of 2026-08-17 two copies of the
  program genuinely exchange a change: one adds a task, the other has it, and when
  both edit the same thing neither edit is lost. What is missing is the part that
  runs it for you in the background and the switch that turns it on. Two promises
  govern it and neither is negotiable: **the server can never read your content**,
  and **if you never turn syncing on, nothing about the app changes** — no network
  is involved at any point.

Further out:

- **Sharing** — sending a note or a board to someone read-only, and a grocery list
  two people can edit at the same time. Both need syncing to exist first.
- **More modules over time** — goals, time tracking, health, car, travel, inventory,
  shopping, read-later, a library, a password vault, and eventually an AI assistant
  that helps organize everything for you.
- **A continuing polish pass.** The founder's verdict after installing the first
  build was that the app read *raw*. Most of what that named has been fixed, and
  finishing quality still outranks adding breadth.

The full list is in [SPECIFICATION.md](SPECIFICATION.md), and exactly what's finished
versus still to do is always kept up to date in [STATUS.md](STATUS.md).

## How it's being built (in plain terms)

- **Desktop first, and that part is done.** The installed computer app was made
  genuinely good before anything went online. There's still no website or online
  account you can use — that is what's being built now, from the *same* work
  rather than started over.
- **One piece at a time, finished properly.** Rather than leaving lots of
  half-finished features around, each part is built completely before moving on.
- **Careful about quality and security.** Every change is checked automatically
  (does it build, does it pass its tests, does it still run end-to-end, does it match
  the design rules) before it's saved. Security is taken seriously from the very
  first line.
- **A consistent, deliberate look.** A lot of care goes into making Nexus *not* look
  like a generic, templated app — it has its own calm, distinctive style in both its
  light and dark themes.

## The direction

Nexus will be **free and fully unlocked while it grows up**, with premium options
later — and the people who joined early keep a big permanent discount. The goal is
simple: replace the pile of scattered apps with one workspace that respects your time
and your privacy, and actually feels good to use every day.
